When you compare vehicle leasing offers in Sri Lanka, you will often see a **flat rate** quoted, while bank loans usually quote a **reducing balance** rate. They look like the same kind of number, but they are not. This guide explains **flat rate vs reducing balance** interest, shows how much the difference can cost you, and how to compare offers fairly. To check an offer now, use our [loan and leasing EMI calculator](/tools/loan-emi-calculator/).

## What is a reducing balance rate?

With a **reducing balance** rate, the lender charges interest each month only on the money you **still owe**.

Your monthly instalment (EMI) stays the same, but the split inside it changes. Early on, much of each payment is interest. As the balance falls, the interest part shrinks and more of your payment goes towards the principal. Most bank personal loans, housing loans and many vehicle loans work this way.

## What is a flat rate?

With a **flat rate**, interest is worked out on the **full amount you borrowed, for the whole term**, as if you never repaid any of it:

- **Total interest** = amount × rate × number of years
- **Monthly instalment** = (amount + total interest) ÷ number of months

It is simple, which is why it is popular in leasing. The catch is that you keep paying interest on money you have already paid back.

### Why the gap is so big

On a reducing balance, by the final year you are only paying interest on a small remaining balance. On a flat rate, you are still paying interest on the full {formatLKR(amount)} in month {months}, even though you have repaid almost all of it.

## How to compare leasing and loan offers

1. **Find out how each rate is quoted.** Ask directly: "Is this a flat rate or a reducing balance rate?" It should be stated in the offer.
2. **Convert to the same type of rate.** Put a flat-rate offer into the calculator with "Flat rate" selected and note the equivalent reducing-balance rate.
3. **Compare like with like.** The offer with the lower reducing-balance rate costs less interest, for the same amount and term.
4. **Add the fees.** Documentation charges, insurance, valuation fees and stamp duty can change which offer is cheaper overall.

## Common mistakes and tips

**Comparing a flat rate with a reducing rate directly.** A lower flat rate can still be far more expensive than a higher reducing-balance rate. Always convert first.

**Focusing only on the monthly instalment.** A longer term lowers the instalment but increases the total interest, especially on a flat rate.

**Forgetting the down payment.** Enter the amount you will actually finance, after the down payment, not the full vehicle price.

**Assuming early settlement saves all the remaining interest.** With flat-rate leases, how much interest you save by settling early depends on the lender's method. Ask for a written settlement quote.

## Conclusion

A flat rate and a reducing-balance rate with the same number are not the same price. Flat-rate interest is charged on the full amount for the whole term, so it can cost close to double the interest of the same reducing-balance rate.
