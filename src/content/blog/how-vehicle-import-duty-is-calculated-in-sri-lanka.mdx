Planning to import a car, SUV, hybrid or EV? The price on the Japanese auction sheet is only the start. By the time the vehicle leaves the port, taxes can add more than the vehicle itself costs. This guide explains how **vehicle import duty in Sri Lanka** is calculated, step by step, so you can understand every line of the bill. To get an estimate for a specific vehicle, use our [vehicle import duty calculator](/tools/vehicle-import-duty-calculator/).

## The starting point: the CIF value

Every duty and tax starts from the **CIF value**. CIF stands for:

- **Cost**: the price you pay for the vehicle
- **Insurance**: the cost of insuring it during shipping
- **Freight**: the cost of shipping it to a Sri Lankan port

Customs converts the CIF value to rupees using its official exchange rate on the day the vehicle is cleared. This matters: if the rupee weakens between the day you buy the vehicle and the day it arrives, your duty goes up even though the vehicle price has not changed.

## How vehicle import duty is calculated, step by step

Sri Lanka charges several different taxes on an imported vehicle. They are calculated **in order**, because most of the later taxes are charged on top of the earlier ones.

### 1. Customs import duty

Customs import duty (often called CID) is a percentage of the CIF value. Depending on the rules in force, a **surcharge** may be added, calculated as a percentage of the customs duty itself.

### 2. Excise duty

Excise duty is usually the largest single tax on a passenger vehicle. It is set by gazette, separately for each fuel type, and depends on:

- **Engine capacity** in cubic centimetres (cc) for petrol, diesel and most hybrid vehicles
- **Motor power** in kilowatts (kW) for electric vehicles
- **The age of the vehicle**, because rates are set in age bands

Excise is often expressed as a fixed amount of rupees **per cc** (or per kW). Some bands may instead use a percentage of the value, or charge whichever of the two is higher.

### 3. Luxury tax

Higher-value vehicles can also pay a **luxury tax**. It applies only when the CIF value is above a threshold, and the threshold can differ between petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric vehicles. Depending on the rule, it is charged on the amount above the threshold or on the whole value.

### 4. SSCL and VAT

The **Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL)** and **Value Added Tax (VAT)** come last. Each is a percentage of a base that is usually made up of the CIF value (often uplifted by a set percentage) plus the duties already charged. This means VAT is effectively charged partly on other taxes, which is one reason the total adds up so quickly.

### 5. Fixed levies

Some charges are a fixed amount per vehicle rather than a percentage. Check the current rules for any levies that apply to your vehicle type.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Using the auction price instead of the CIF value.** Insurance and freight are part of the value that duty is charged on. Leaving them out makes your estimate too low.

**Forgetting about the exchange rate.** A few rupees' movement in the yen or dollar rate changes the CIF value in rupees, and every percentage-based tax moves with it. Use the current Central Bank rate and leave yourself a margin.

**Entering the wrong engine capacity.** Excise bands have hard edges. A vehicle just under a band limit and one just over it can pay very different rates. Use the exact figure from the export certificate.

**Ignoring the vehicle's age.** Excise rates depend on age, and there may be a maximum age for imports. Check the year of manufacture before you commit.

**Choosing the wrong fuel type.** Petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric vehicles each have their own tables and luxury tax thresholds. A hybrid entered as a petrol car will give the wrong answer.

**Treating the estimate as the final bill.** Clearing agent fees, port and handling charges, and registration costs come on top of the taxes. Budget for them separately.

## Where the rates come from

Vehicle excise rates are set by gazette under the Excise (Special Provisions) Act, and customs duty, surcharges and levies are set by the government and administered by [Sri Lanka Customs](https://www.customs.gov.lk/). These rates change often, sometimes with very little notice.

## Conclusion

Vehicle import duty in Sri Lanka builds up in layers: customs duty and any surcharge on the CIF value, excise based on engine size and age, luxury tax on expensive vehicles, and finally SSCL and VAT charged on top of everything before them. Understanding that order makes it much easier to see why two similar-looking vehicles can have very different bills.
