## What is APIT?

**APIT** stands for **Advance Personal Income Tax**. It is the income tax your employer deducts from your pay each month and sends to the **Inland Revenue Department (IRD)**. You may still hear it called **PAYE** (Pay As You Earn), the name of the older scheme.

Your employer calculates it using tables published by the IRD. For regular monthly pay (salary, allowances and taxable benefits) from your main job, they use **Tax Table No. 01**.

## How APIT tax is calculated in Sri Lanka

### Step 1: Work out your gross regular pay

APIT is charged on your **regular profits from employment**. That includes your salary plus allowances such as travelling, fuel or overtime pay, and the value of taxable non-cash benefits. Your own EPF contribution is **not** subtracted first. Tax is worked out on the full gross amount.

### Step 2: Take off the personal relief

Everyone gets a tax-free **personal relief** of <strong>{formatLKR(relief)}</strong> a year, which the IRD table spreads evenly as <strong>{formatLKR(relief / 12)}</strong> a month. If your monthly pay is at or below that, no APIT is deducted.

### Step 3: Apply the tax bands

Pay above the relief is taxed in bands. Each rate applies **only to the slice of pay inside its band**, never to your whole salary. Here are the monthly bands currently used by the IRD:

Because only the extra rupees fall into a higher band, a pay rise can never leave you with less take-home pay than before.

## Where EPF and ETF come in

Two other deductions and contributions appear on most payslips:

- **EPF (Employees' Provident Fund):** you contribute <strong>{formatPercent(rates.epf.employeeRate)}</strong> of your earnings, which is deducted from your pay, and your employer adds <strong>{formatPercent(rates.epf.employerRate)}</strong>.
- **ETF (Employees' Trust Fund):** your employer pays <strong>{formatPercent(rates.etf.employerRate)}</strong>. Nothing is deducted from you.

EPF and ETF are worked out on your "total earnings" for EPF purposes. That is often your basic salary, sometimes plus certain allowances, so check your payslip to see which amounts your employer includes.

## Common mistakes and tips

**Applying the top rate to your whole salary.** Moving into a higher band only affects the rupees above that band's starting point. Your overall tax rate is always lower than your top band rate.

**Taking EPF off before working out tax.** APIT is charged on gross pay. Your EPF contribution comes out separately and does not reduce the tax.

**Forgetting allowances.** Fuel, travelling and most other allowances are taxable and count towards APIT, even if they are not part of your basic salary.

**Using the monthly table for bonuses.** Bonuses, arrears and other lump-sum payments are taxed using different IRD tables (such as Table No. 02), so a month with a bonus will not match the regular calculation.

**Ignoring a mid-year job change.** If you join or leave a job partway through the year, your employer uses Table No. 05 based on cumulative pay, so the deduction can differ for those months.

The bands and relief above come from the Inland Revenue Department's [APIT tax tables](https://www.ird.gov.lk/en/publications/sitepages/apit_tax_tables.aspx?menuid=1502), which apply the Inland Revenue Act as amended. ## Conclusion

APIT is simpler than it looks: start from your gross regular pay, take off the monthly personal relief, and tax each slice of the remainder at its band's rate. Your EPF contribution is deducted separately, and your employer adds EPF and ETF on top of your salary.
