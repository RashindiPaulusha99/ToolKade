Your electricity bill can feel unpredictable: use a few more units and the total jumps far more than you expected. This guide explains **how your CEB electricity bill is calculated**, from the tariff blocks to the fixed charge, with worked examples. To check your own bill, use our [CEB and LECO electricity bill calculator](/tools/electricity-bill-calculator/).

## Who sets the tariff?

Electricity tariffs in Sri Lanka are approved by the **Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka (PUCSL)**. The current tariff took effect on <strong>{formatDate(ratesFile.effectiveFrom)}</strong>, and it applies to retail customers of both the **Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB)** and **Lanka Electricity Company (LECO)**.

## How your CEB electricity bill is calculated

Your bill has two parts: an **energy charge** for the units (kWh) you used, and a monthly **fixed charge**.

### Step 1: Find your units

Your units are the **current meter reading minus the previous reading**. Both readings are printed on your bill.

### Step 2: Find your band

The domestic tariff has different rate tables depending on your **total monthly use**:

- up to <strong>{lowLimit}</strong> units a month,
- <strong>{lowLimit + 1}–{highLimit}</strong> units a month,
- more than <strong>{highLimit}</strong> units a month.

Which table applies depends on your total, and **all** your units are then charged using that table. This is the key to understanding big jumps in your bill.

### Step 3: Charge each block

Within the table, units are charged in **blocks**. Here are the current domestic rates for a 30-day billing period:

### Step 4: Add the fixed charge

The **fixed charge** depends on the highest block your usage reaches. Add it to the energy charge to get your bill.

### The {highLimit}-unit jump

Compare <strong>{highLimit}</strong> units and <strong>{highLimit + 1}</strong> units: one extra unit raises the bill from <strong>{formatLKR(bill(highLimit).total)}</strong> to <strong>{formatLKR(bill(highLimit + 1).total)}</strong>. That is because crossing the limit moves **every** unit onto the higher rate table and raises the fixed charge. If your usage is close to a limit, cutting back a few units can save a lot.

## Common mistakes and tips

**Looking only at the block rates.** The band you fall into changes the rate for all your units, not just the extra ones. Check where your total sits against the limits.

**Ignoring the billing period.** A 33-day bill covers more units than a 30-day one, but PUCSL prorates the blocks, so the limits stretch a little too. Enter the actual number of days for an accurate estimate.

**Comparing prepaid and postpaid rates directly.** LECO's optional prepaid scheme has different blocks and no fixed charge, so work out your typical monthly bill under both.

**Leaving appliances on standby.** Small loads add up across a month. Switching off at the plug is an easy way to stay below a limit.

## Conclusion

Your CEB or LECO bill is the energy charge for your units, worked out block by block from the rate table for your monthly total, plus a fixed charge for the highest block you reach. The biggest surprises come from crossing a limit such as {highLimit} units, which moves all your units onto higher rates.
